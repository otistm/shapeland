# Influences: Miyazaki, Kojima, Geometry

Written to be **applied and tested**, not admired. Every rule below is falsifiable against a
playtest or a proof.

---

## PART A — Hidetaka Miyazaki / FromSoftware

### Stated philosophy, in his own words

- **Difficulty is instrumental, never the point.** Demon's Souls, 2009: "Having the game be
  'difficult' was never the goal. What we set out to do was strictly to provide a sense of
  accomplishment. We understood that 'difficulty' is just one way to offer an intense sense of
  accomplishment through forming strategies, overcoming obstacles, and discovering new things."
  Later, bluntly: "I personally want my games to be described as satisfying rather than difficult."
- **The twin pillars.** Dark Souls, 2011: "The concepts behind Dark Souls are 'sense of
  achievement' and 'surprise of discovery.'" Elden Ring adds a third: "we placed major focus on a
  'sense of adventure'."
- **Origins in unreadable books.** He grew up poor in Shizuoka, borrowing library books beyond his
  reading level: "I found so much joy in those stories. It was a rich reading experience, even if I
  wasn't always reading." The method follows: "The stories were fragmented and filled with
  mysteries... But I enjoyed putting the pieces together and filling the gaps with my
  imagination." Then the thesis: **"Dark Souls is in a way incomplete. I want players to complete
  it with their discoveries."**
- **Trust as an axiom.** GDC 2022, asked how to design confusing zones: "Trust players. Players
  will figure out what to do next."

### Information design — what is withheld vs what is always fair

Withheld: quest markers, objective lists, maps in dungeons, explicit causality. **Guaranteed fair:
hitboxes, telegraph timing, enemy placement legibility, and the fact that every death is
diagnostic.** He withholds *interpretation*, never information the body needs to survive.

**Difficulty substitutes for signposting.** "I thought it would be fun for players to go into the
wrong stage and get brutally beaten up so badly that they would automatically know that the stage
was not the one to go at that point." Combat is the compass.

**Lore lives in objects.** FromSoftware's Yasuhiro Kitao: "the story is told in fragments, and we
didn't deviate from our idea of giving the player a world and a narrative to interpret freely."
Miyazaki wrote the item texts himself.

**Asynchronous multiplayer as regulated loneliness.** Messages and bloodstains exist to produce "a
loose feeling of connections" — deliberately not friend lists. Producer Takeshi Miyazoe: "the
sharing of the loneliness with other people, so that you get that faint sense of comfort when you
feel that another player's facing the same difficulties... without being able to have direct
contact." Note the constraint that makes it work: **soapstone messages are built from a fixed
template grammar**, so authorship is expressive but bounded. The ambiguity is engineered.

### Encounter and boss design

- **First death is information.** 2011: "when a player dies, we try to leave a sense of 'maybe if I
  try a different strategy I can succeed.'"
- **High attack, low defense, on both sides.** "We don't want users to hack and hack and hack
  away... It's more strategic." Lethality shortens the retry loop and makes avoidance, not
  attrition, the skill.
- **Contradiction in boss character.** 2015: "One of the elements I want to bring to boss character
  design is contradiction. We have the Dancer of the Frigid Valley; she is definitely a formidable
  enemy, but at the same time players sense not only that it's scary, but there's a sense of
  sadness." Also the **heat-up system** — mid-fight tactical mutation, not a second health bar.
- **Risk economy.** Dropped souls are recoverable exactly once, on site: "if the Souls could be
  recovered anytime, there would be no suspense or sense of accomplishment."
- **Bosses are placed before the map exists.** "Our first step when designing the map was to decide
  where the bosses would appear... then we adjusted the boss gameplay based on their location."

### World structure

The signature reward is not loot but **topology** — a kicked-down ladder that folds a two-hour
journey into a ten-second shortcut, re-scoring everything already explored. Verticality over
horizontality; areas stacked above and below; loading minimized so space feels continuous and
therefore trustworthy.

### Aesthetic principles

Melancholy grandeur: architecture built for a civilization that no longer exists at the scale it
built for. Decay implies prior magnificence, which implies a story you were not told. Scale is both
threat and waypoint. Silence is the default ambience and music is punctuation. Grotesque forms are
shot with reverence, so revulsion and pity coexist.

### Extractable rules

1. Never tune for difficulty; tune for the moment of accomplishment. If a hard thing produces no
   click of mastery, cut it.
2. Make death cheap in time and expensive in resources, and make the resource recoverable exactly
   once, in place.
3. Guarantee every death was preceded by information the player could have read. Audit every death
   for a legible tell.
4. Teach with the body first: the second encounter with a pattern must be survivable using only
   what the first taught.
5. Use lethality, not health bars, to create tension.
6. Signpost with danger, not markers. Wrong direction = escalating punishment, not a locked door.
7. Never explain in text what a room can demonstrate.
8. Put lore in objects and geometry. Fragments only, and let fragments contradict each other.
9. Ship the world incomplete on purpose; leave gaps sized for imagination, not confusion.
10. Give every major enemy one contradiction (fearsome + pitiable, ornate + broken).
11. Change tactics mid-fight, not just numbers.
12. Design boss positions first; grow the map outward from them.
13. Build loops, not corridors: every long path should eventually reveal a shortcut home.
14. Make the shortcut the reward. Topological revelation beats loot.
15. Prefer vertical stacking so players can see where they've been and where they're going.
16. Default to silence; spend music only on punctuation.
17. Let players leave bounded, template-limited messages — expression without spoilers.
18. Let ambient traces of other players be visible; keep direct contact rare.
19. Never place an ambush unsurvivable on first contact with correct reflexes.
20. Trust the player publicly, and design as if they will read the room.

## PART B — Hideo Kojima

### Stated philosophy

- **Stick and rope.** The source is Kōbō Abe's short story *"Nawa" ("The Rope")*: "'The Rope' and
  'The Stick,' together, are one of humankind's oldest 'tools.' 'The Stick' is for keeping evil
  away; 'The Rope' is for pulling good toward us." Kojima, 2016: "Most of your tools in action games
  are sticks. You punch or you shoot or you kick... In [Death Stranding], I want people to be
  connected not through sticks, but through what would be the equivalent of ropes... But of course
  you will be able to use the sticks too."
- **Important caveat.** In 2025 Kojima said the thesis came *after* the concept: "The idea for
  DEATH STRANDING began with the image of Sam walking alone through a wasteland... The concept of
  the rope and stick came after the fact." It is a retrofitted articulation of an image —
  legitimate practice, but do not present it as the design's origin.
- **"Hiding is the fun" was born from a constraint.** MSX2 could not display enough sprites for
  combat, so he conceived "a combat game without fighting," then a game about running and hiding,
  then reframed it as infiltration to make it heroic. **The stealth genre is a hardware limit
  turned into a verb.**
- **Agency vs authorship — he chooses authorship, knowingly.** "There's the multi-ending story
  technique and that's a technique I'm not very interested in. I hope to make video games where the
  player's destiny is set." His stated ideal is an "illusion of freedom, but also story."

### Systemic design

Stealth as a **legibility contract**: vision cones, patrol routes, alert states and sound radii are
all inspectable, so the player forms and tests hypotheses. AI must be dumb in *explainable* ways.
Gadgets are verbs with side effects, each opening a solution space rather than solving one puzzle.

**Social Strand System:** ladders, ropes, bridges and signs left by other players persist;
contributed resources build shared roads; structures decay unless maintained; footpaths physically
form where enough players walked. Reward is *likes* — deliberately non-convertible. Kojima, 2025:
"Likes in the game don't have a physical impact – they can't be converted to currency, nor do they
make you stronger. Yet it feels good when you receive a like."

**Terrain as antagonist:** cargo mass, stack height, centre of gravity, slope, footing, water depth
and stamina make *walking* the challenge. The stick/rope logic applies to traversal itself — the
ladder is a rope.

**Systems should be able to critique themselves.** After COVID: "I started ruminating over the
dangers of being 'too connected.'"

### Presentation craft

Cinema is the native grammar — 1997: "For Metal Gear, too, I'm trying to get as close to film as
possible." He is self-aware about the cost, wanting to avoid "those tedious scenes where characters
are just blabbering at each other for 4 or 5 minutes." His stated goal: "When you've finished Metal
Gear, I want you to feel like 'ah... I understand now.'" **Compare Miyazaki's exactly opposite
target.**

Fourth-wall breaks as mechanics: Psycho Mantis reading the memory card and requiring the controller
be moved to port 2; the codec as diegetic UI; The End's sniper duel affected by the system clock.
Interruption and tonal whiplash are deliberate — comedy adjacent to atrocity, long silence before
violence. UI is a diegetic object the character carries and can lose.

### Sound as mechanic

The alert stinger is the most efficient state-communication device in stealth games: it names the
state, sets the timer, and changes the music simultaneously. Alert → Evasion → Caution → Clear is
*audible* before it is visible. Silence is the resting state that makes the stinger legible; sound
is also an input.

### Extractable rules

1. Take your hardest constraint and make it the genre.
2. Give the player a rope for every stick; make cooperation mechanical, not moral.
3. Reward prosocial acts with non-convertible currency, so the reward stays social.
4. Let other players' leftovers persist and require maintenance.
5. Let repeated collective traversal physically alter terrain.
6. Make hostile intelligence legible enough to experiment on; publish the rules through behavior.
7. Make avoidance a first-class win condition with its own scoring.
8. Model traversal as a skill: weight, balance, footing, momentum.
9. Give every gadget a cost and an unintended use.
10. Communicate state changes with sound first, UI second.
11. Keep silence as the default so a single stinger can restructure the scene.
12. Put the UI inside the fiction; let it be damaged, lost, or upgraded.
13. Author the destiny; improvise the route. One ending, many paths.
14. Stage at least one moment that reaches outside the game's frame.
15. Use tonal whiplash on purpose: place levity next to dread to sharpen both.
16. Break your own theme later — turn the thesis into a question.
17. Introduce an antagonist *system* (weather, terrain, time), not only antagonist characters.
18. Make the opening a promise of tone, not a tutorial.

## PART C — Synthesis for a geometric game

### Where they conflict, and how to hold both

The core conflict is **who narrates**. Kojima's stated aim is "ah... I understand now"; Miyazaki's
is "Dark Souls is in a way incomplete." One resolves; the other refuses. Secondary conflict: Kojima
interrupts play to stage meaning, Miyazaki treats uninterrupted embodiment as sacred.

Reconciliation that does not produce mush:

- **Split them by layer, not by scene.** Miyazaki governs the *moment-to-moment*: no markers, no
  tutorials, silence, fragments, death-as-information. Kojima governs *structure and punctuation*:
  authored openings, staged arrivals, one fixed destiny, alert-state audio, systemic generosity
  between players. **Never let Kojima's camera take the controller mid-encounter.**
- **Interrupt only at thresholds** — boss arrival, region entry, first contact with a new shape
  class. Miyazaki does this too (fog gate, music cue); he just does it without dialogue. Use
  staging, not speech.
- **Kojima's ropes, Miyazaki's silence.** Asynchronous help can be lavish as long as it is
  *wordless and bounded* — a fixed glyph vocabulary satisfies both directors at once.
- **One ending, many routes** satisfies Kojima; **unexplained ending** satisfies Miyazaki. Fixed
  destiny, withheld meaning.

### Expressing both in form, height, shadow, and earned color

- **Shape *is* the telegraph.** Circles read approachable, squares stable, triangles aggressive.
  Make it a contract: a form's face count, sharpness, and symmetry must predict its behavior on
  first sight, and **never lie**. This is Miyazaki's readable telegraph and Kojima's legible AI in
  the same object.
- **Contradiction via geometry, not faces.** A boss reads as formidable *and* sad if it is
  asymmetric: a broken vertex, a missing face, a slow off-beat wobble in an otherwise perfect
  solid. Damage its Platonic ideal and it reads as tragedy with no text.
- **Height is lore and grammar.** Verticality does triple duty: shortcut-revelation (drop down and
  land at the hub), threat scaling (silhouettes on the skyline), and information — what you can see
  from a high place is the only quest log.
- **Shadow is sound design's visual twin.** On a white grid, shadow is the only cheap
  high-contrast signal. Use shadow *length* and *sharpness* for windup timing, shadow *presence*
  for offscreen threats, and loss of shadow for the uncanny.
- **Color as the souls economy.** Because color is scarce it can be proof of accomplishment, a
  mnemonic map, a player-authored message, and the only permitted UI at once. A colored trace left
  by another player is literally Kojima's ladder and Miyazaki's bloodstain in one object.
- **Rolling as the body of the rules.** 90° commitment per input means every mistake is spatial and
  legible. Heavy faces, chipped edges and slope momentum give Kojima's terrain-as-antagonist for
  free.
- **Text budget:** treat item lore as *glyph* description — a small icon set whose meaning
  accretes. Ambiguity should come from combination, not vagueness.

### Precedents worth studying

- **Thomas Was Alone** — the strongest direct precedent: personality derived from shape + ability +
  size, and squash/stretch alone makes rectangles read as bodies. "Of course the tall shape can
  jump higher." One dependency Shapeland does not have: a narrator.
- **Journey** — triangular robe forms echoed across the landscape, so character and world share a
  shape language; posture carries emotion with no face. Also the model for wordless asynchronous
  companionship.
- **Shadow of the Colossus** — silhouette and scale as characterization; each boss is a level, and
  melancholy comes from architecture-as-creature.
- **Rez / Tetris Effect** — abstract form bound to audio state; the model for making sound the
  primary readability channel.
- **Luxo Jr.** (Pixar, 1986) — a faceless lamp achieving full character through motion timing
  alone. The canonical proof that behavior beats features.
- **Chuck Jones' *The Dot and the Line*** and **Abbott's *Flatland*** — geometry as dramatis
  personae. *(Real works; not source-verified.)*
- **Bauhaus / Kandinsky shape-color correspondence** (triangle–yellow, square–red, circle–blue) — a
  ready-made system for tying earned color to form class. *(Historically documented; not verified
  here.)*
